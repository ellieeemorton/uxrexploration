# Transcript P9 — Oceanside Loan Servicing, Repayment Plan Comparison Study

Session: 45 min, remote video call, screen-share
Participant: P9 — 44, warehouse operations supervisor, about $29,000 remaining on loans originally about $21,000, Oceanside customer for thirteen years
Moderator: M

---

## Part 1: Background

**M:** Okay, so that's recording. Hi P9, thanks for making the time. Could you start by telling me a bit about your loans?

**P9:** Yeah. Okay. So I borrowed twenty-one thousand. Twenty-one. In, what, two thousand eight, two thousand nine. And I have paid on this thing for thirteen years. And I owe twenty-nine thousand. Explain that to me. You tell me how a person borrows twenty-one and, after thirteen years of paying, owes more than they borrowed. I'll wait.

**M:** That sounds really frustrating. Was there a period where payments were paused, or?

**P9:** Yeah, there was a forbearance, a couple, when I got laid off in, when the plant closed. They said, don't worry, you don't have to pay, and then the interest just, piled up, and they put it on the balance. They capitalized it, that's the word. I looked it up. So I'm paying interest on interest. That's legal, apparently.

**M:** Right. So, I want to make sure I understand, um, how often are you on the Oceanside site?

**P9:** Every month, to pay. And every month I look at the balance and it makes me want to throw the laptop. It goes down like, what, eighty dollars. I pay three-something and it goes down eighty. Because the rest is interest. Every month. It's a joke.

**M:** Okay, yeah. And, um, have you ever compared plans on the site?

**P9:** Once, a few years ago. I was looking for anything. And I remember I got to the end and I thought, this is just rearranging the same bad deal. It's like, which chair do you want on the Titanic.

**M:** Ha. Okay. Well, uh, let's, let me get you to share your screen, and we'll go to the comparison page together.

**P9:** Sure. Go ahead.

## Part 2: Screen-share task

[P9 shares screen. He logs in. Dashboard loads. P9 looks at the balance figure on the dashboard and stays on it for about ten seconds.]

**P9:** See that? Twenty-nine thousand, four hundred and some. That's the number. That's the number I'd like somebody to explain.

**M:** Right. Um, if you could go to Compare Plans, actually, for the task.

[P9 clicks "Repayment," then "Compare Plans."]

**P9:** Okay. Table.

[Table loads. P9 reads for about fifteen seconds. Scrolls a little, then stops.]

**P9:** So Standard, three-fifty. Payoff date, let me see, 2033, hm. So I'm going to be paying this thing for twenty-five years total. For a twenty-one thousand loan. That's, that's almost a quarter century for a car's worth of money.

**M:** What stands out to you on the table?

**P9:** The payoff date. Because that's the part that makes me mad. No, sorry, it's not the table. It's just the situation. The table's fine. It says what it says.

**M:** Okay, and in terms of comparing the plans, uh, what do you look at?

**P9:** I mean, I look at the payment. But then, what's it matter. All of them have me paying way more than I borrowed. The lowest total is, what, I'd have to look. Forty-one thousand. On a twenty-one-thousand loan. Forty-one! It's, the whole system's rigged, that's, I'm not, I don't say that lightly, but.

[P9 hovers over "Total estimated paid" for the Standard row. Clicks the "i" icon. Tooltip appears. He reads it.]

**P9:** "Includes projected interest." Yeah, I'll bet it does. That's the interest that I already paid three times over.

**M:** Do you trust that number?

**P9:** I don't trust anything they tell me. Not after the capitalization thing. Nobody told me about that when it happened. You find out later, in the mail, in some letter written in legalese. So when they show me a projected total, I think, projected. What are you going to do to it between now and then?

**M:** Okay. That's, that's a, yeah. What would make you trust it more?

**P9:** Honestly? If they told me what the rate is. On this page, right next to the plans. The interest rate. It's not even on here. I'm looking. Hm, no, it's not. It's the main thing! Every plan has the same rate, presumably, but why isn't it on here.

**M:** That's interesting. Do you think, uh, that would change how you compare?

**P9:** No. It wouldn't change anything. The rate is what it is. I just want to see it. It'd at least feel honest.

[P9 scrolls down to the income estimator. Does not click in it. Scrolls back up.]

**M:** Did you want to try the income box?

**P9:** I know my income. I don't need a box for that. I'm a supervisor, I make what I make. It's not going to, you know, it's not going to tell me anything I don't know.

**M:** Okay. So which of the plans would you pick?

**P9:** Standard. I can't afford to pay more per month and the other ones take me longer, which means more interest, which means more of this. I stay where I am, and I'm still mad.

[P9 clicks "Select plan" on the Standard row. Confirmation page loads. He reads the first paragraph, scrolls, stops. Clicks "Back."]

**P9:** That's fine. It's clear. I'm not going to argue with that page.

**M:** I, yeah, I can't speak to that. Uh, thank you, P9. Anything else you want to share?

**P9:** Just that I've been paying for thirteen years and I'm about eight thousand dollars further in the hole than where I started. You can put that in your report. I'd like someone at a desk somewhere to know that.

**M:** I'll make sure it's noted. Thanks so much.

**P9:** Yeah. Thanks for letting me vent. Sorry.
