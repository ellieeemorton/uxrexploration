# Transcript P6 — Oceanside Loan Servicing, Repayment Plan Comparison Study

Session: 45 min, remote video call, screen-share
Participant: P6 — 47, HVAC dispatcher, about $22,000 remaining (old undergraduate loans, consolidated), Oceanside customer for nine years
Moderator: M

---

## Part 1: Background

**M:** Okay, good, it's recording. So, uh, thanks for doing this. Why don't you start by telling me a bit about your loan situation?

**P6:** Yeah, so, I went back to school at, like, thirty-something, part time, while working. Community college and then a four-year. Consolidated it all into one thing a few years back. I owe, I wanna say twenty-two? Twenty-two thousand and some change. It's supposed to be done in, I don't know, six, seven years.

**M:** And what plan are you on?

**P6:** The regular one. Fixed payment. Not fancy.

**M:** Okay. How do you usually keep track of it? Like, how do you check where you're at?

**P6:** Oh, I don't really use the website for that. Every month they send the paper statement, and I just look at that. Kitchen counter, it comes in, I open it, I look at the balance at the top and I file it in the folder. I've done that since, I don't know, forever. I'm old school. I don't trust the online thing to, you know, remember anything. Paper's paper.

**M:** Got it. So you don't log in much?

**P6:** Hardly ever. Maybe to change my bank info. That's about it.

**M:** And do you ever call them, the servicer?

**P6:** Couple times. Mostly when autopay messed up. The phone tree is, you know how it is, press one, press two, and then you get a person and they ask for your whole life story. But the people are nice enough. One lady stayed on the line with me twenty minutes one time, I remember that. That's the kind of thing that sticks.

**M:** Okay. And have you ever compared plans?

**P6:** Once or twice. My wife made me. She read an article about how you can pay less if you switch. So I looked, and, honestly, I couldn't tell if it was worth it, so I dropped it.

**M:** Do you remember why you couldn't tell?

**P6:** I don't know. Too many numbers. Then the dispatcher phone rang, literally, I was at work, and I never came back to it.

**M:** Ha. Okay. Alright, well, let's try it fresh. I'll have you share your screen, and go to the Compare Plans page.

---

## Part 2: Screen-share task

[P6 shares screen. Logs in. The password manager autofills on the second try. Dashboard loads.]

**P6:** Okay. There's my number right there, see? Top of the page. Twenty-two thousand, one-forty-something. I see that every time I log in. I check that pretty regular, actually, it's right on the front.

[P6 looks at the dashboard balance for about three seconds, then clicks "Repayment" and "Compare Plans."]

**M:** Okay. What do you see on this page?

[Comparison page loads. P6 scrolls down the table. Stops.]

**P6:** Alright, so four plans. Standard is what I got, three-forty. There's a, this one, the Graduated, it starts at like two-forty and climbs. I've heard about those, they're a trap if your pay doesn't go up.

**M:** Why do you say a trap?

**P6:** Because the starting number's low and it makes you feel great, and then a couple years in you're paying more than you would've, and you didn't plan for it. A guy I work with did that with his truck, not loans, but same idea.

**M:** What are you looking at now?

[P6 is hovering over the Income-Linked Flex row.]

**P6:** This one. Income-Linked. It says a hundred ninety something. That's almost half. But I don't see what it's based on. I'd want to know that.

**M:** Is there anything on the page that tells you?

**P6:** Let me look.

[P6 scrolls down. Finds the income estimator. Reads it for several seconds. Types 64000, household size 4. Clicks "Update estimate."]

**P6:** There we go. Okay, now it's, what, two-ninety. Huh. That's not half anymore. So the first number was wrong, basically.

**M:** Was it wrong, or just an example?

**P6:** I don't know, it didn't say it was an example. To me it looked like my number. Somebody could make a real bad call off that.

[P6 scrolls up. Reads the table again, reading the total estimated paid column down. His lips move slightly.]

**P6:** The total for the long one, Extended 25, forty-something thousand. Yeah, no. I'm not doing that. I'd be paying for a car's worth of interest.

**M:** Okay, and so what, what's your take on the numbers overall, do you trust them?

**P6:** The standard one, yeah, because I know it. That matches. I looked at my own balance right there, and the payoff date for Standard is, um, I think 2032. My paper statement says the same. So I'm kinda okay with it.

**M:** Great. When did you last check that statement?

**P6:** It, uh, it came a couple weeks ago. It's in the folder.

[P6 hovers over the tooltip on "Total estimated paid." Reads it, says nothing. Moves the cursor down to the "Select plan" buttons. Hovers over the Graduated button for about five seconds.]

**P6:** Part of me's still curious about this one. But no. No.

**M:** What draws you to it?

**P6:** The low payment. I'm not gonna lie. It's two-forty. I'd feel it in my pocket right away, every month. It's that "right away" thing. But I know better.

[P6 clicks "Select plan" on the Standard row. Reads the confirmation screen for about ten seconds. Clicks "Back."]

**P6:** Yeah, that's fine. I'd stick with mine.

## Part 3: Follow-up

**M:** So, um, let's talk about what you'd change. If anything.

**P6:** The example number thing, that's the main one. If the top number's a placeholder, say so. Big. Not in tiny type. The other thing, I guess, is that the table assumes I know what all of these are. "Income-Linked Flex." What does that mean in English? I got it eventually, but I had to think.

**M:** And what do you look at first, when you're comparing?

**P6:** The monthly. Then how long it'll take. Then total, if I'm feeling brave. Ha.

**M:** Why brave?

**P6:** Because the total's scary. It's like looking at how much you're paying for a house. I kind of don't want to know.

**M:** That's a good way to put it. Um, anything you want to add about how you keep an eye on your loan in general? Or, you know, where your balance stands?

**P6:** Not really. I mean, I just look at the, yeah, the main screen. When I log in. It's on the front.

**M:** Okay, fair enough. One last thing, how would you feel if there was a feature where the tool asked you a few questions and then suggested a plan?

**P6:** I'd probably look at it. But I'd want to see the table, too. I don't want it hiding stuff. If they just say "we picked this for you," that's, I don't know, that's like a salesman. If they show me why, then okay.

**M:** Makes sense. Is there anything you'd worry about if the tool picked for you?

**P6:** That it's picking what's best for them. Not me. Like I said, salesman. I've had a guy sell me a service plan on a furnace that I didn't need, and ever since, I'm, uh, a little allergic to that. Though, to be fair, a lot of folks would probably like it. My wife would. She'd go, "just tell me what to do."

**M:** Makes sense. Well, thank you so much, this was great.

**P6:** Yeah, no problem. I hope it helps. Tell your boss to fix that example number thing.

**M:** Ha, I will. Thanks.

[Session ends. Total time approximately 29 minutes.]
