# Transcript P2 — Oceanside Loan Servicing, Repayment Plan Comparison Study

Session: 50 min, remote video call, screen-share
Participant: P2 — 52, self-employed electrician, roughly $58,000 remaining (loans taken out for his own schooling, then a parent-side loan for his daughter), Oceanside customer since 2012
Moderator: M

---

## Part 1: Background

**M:** Alright, recording. Thanks, um, for making the time. So to start, could you tell me about your loans, sort of the basics?

**P2:** The basics. Okay. The basics are I borrowed, what was it, about thirty-one thousand, for a trade program and then some of the electrical certification stuff, back when I was like, thirty-six, thirty-seven. And then I took one out for my daughter's first two years because, you know, you do that for your kid. And I've been paying on these things for a decade and the number barely moves. Barely. I want you to write that down.

**M:** I'm, yeah, I'm taking notes. So what's the rate on those?

**P2:** Seven-point-four on the older one. Seven-point-four! You know what my truck loan is? Four. You know what my mortgage is? Three and a quarter. And the government, the government, gets to charge me seven-point-four on something I borrowed to, to make me a taxpayer. It's, it's insane, is what it is. Whoever thought that was okay.

**M:** That sounds really frustrating.

**P2:** Frustrating's not the word. I've paid, I did the math once, I've paid them probably thirty-some thousand already and I still owe more than half of what I borrowed. How does that work? That's not a loan, that's a subscription.

**M:** Okay. And, uh, how do you use the Oceanside site?

**P2:** I log in to make a payment when autopay breaks, which it does, because they change the bank thing every couple years and don't tell you. Then I look at the balance and I get mad and I log out.

**M:** Ha, okay. Have you looked at changing plans?

**P2:** Yeah, like twice. Both times I got so irritated I quit before the end. Not because of the site, honestly, because of the, you know, the numbers. You look at it and it's like, oh, I could pay less a month and only pay another twenty grand total. Great. Fantastic. That's a real deal.

**M:** Okay. So, let's get you into the screen-share and we'll try it. I'll ask you to go to the compare plans page and just talk through it.

---

## Part 2: Screen-share task

[P2 shares screen. Oceanside dashboard is visible. P2 has a lot of browser tabs open. He closes two while the moderator is speaking.]

**P2:** Okay, so where do I go. Repayment, I guess.

[P2 clicks "Repayment." Dropdown appears. P2 clicks "Compare Plans" on the first try.]

**P2:** Here we go. So this is the table. Four plans. Standard, Graduated, Income-Linked Flex, Extended 25. Who names these.

[Table loads. P2 looks at the monthly payment column and stops. He does not scroll. About fifteen seconds with the cursor not moving.]

**P2:** Three-ninety a month on the standard. That's almost exactly what I'm paying. Okay. And, and look at this, total estimated paid, eighty-one thousand. I borrowed fifty, what's left is fifty-eight, and they're telling me I'm going to pay eighty-one more. That's twenty-three thousand dollars of pure, it's just interest. It's the seven-four. It's all the seven-four.

**M:** Right. And, um, is that, the total, is that something you looked at first?

**P2:** I looked at it first because it's the one that makes me sick. The monthly I already know.

[P2 scrolls slowly down the page. Stops at the income estimator box. Does not click it. Scrolls back up.]

**P2:** You know what gets me? I've got a neighbor, he's a dentist, he has a fixed rate from a bank on his practice loan, and his rate is like five. And he's a dentist. He's got, you know, a whole different situation. And I'm out here on a ladder, and I'm getting hit with seven percent. It's the people who can least afford it.

**M:** Mm. Yeah. Can we, maybe look at the plans themselves? Like, what stands out between them?

**P2:** The long one, Extended 25, it's a hundred and thirty-something a month. Which, sure, that'd help some months. Some months I don't have any work, it's winter, nobody's calling me. But then the total is, what, a hundred and twelve thousand. No. Are you kidding me. I'd be paying for a house for a loan that's for a certificate.

[P2 hovers over the "Total estimated paid" header. Tooltip appears. He reads it, lips moving slightly.]

**P2:** "Includes projected interest." Okay. Sure it does. What does it mean, projected? Projected based on what? Because I'll tell you what, I'm self-employed, my income jumps around like crazy. One year I make ninety, the next year I make fifty-five. So when I see a number for the Income-Linked one, and it says a hundred and forty-something a month, I'm going, based on what? It doesn't say what income it assumed. It doesn't say if it's last year's or some average or what. And if they're going to recalculate every year, then the total at the bottom is, it's made up. It's a guess dressed up as a number. If it just said, this assumes you earn X, I'd at least know how much to trust it. Right now I don't.

**M:** That's, that's really useful actually. Um, so you'd want that assumption shown?

**P2:** Sure. Whatever. But that's not the thing. The thing is that the rate is seven-four and there's nothing on this page that lets me do anything about the rate. You know what I want? I want a button that says "lower my rate." And there isn't one. You can shuffle the payments around all day, but the rate's the rate.

**M:** Right, yeah, that's outside what this page does. Um, let's, let me ask, if you were going to choose one, which would you look at?

**P2:** I wouldn't choose. I'd be, I'd sit here and stew. That's what I did the last time. Actually, let me tell you what I did the last time. I looked at this same thing, and then I went and I looked up what the interest actually was on the statement, and then I got on the phone with a guy at, at a call center, and he was reading off a script, and he goes, "Sir, the rate is set by the terms of your note." Like I don't know that.

[P2 clicks "Select plan" on the Graduated row. Confirmation page appears. P2 reads for a few seconds, clicks "Back" without submitting.]

**P2:** Yeah. It's fine. It says what it says.

**M:** Was there anything about that page you'd say was confusing?

**P2:** Not confusing. Just, it's, it's a menu of bad options. That's not the page's fault, though, I'll give you that.

## Part 3: Follow-up

**M:** Okay. Uh, so to wrap up, what would you say you look at first on that page, really?

**P2:** The total. The big number at the end. I look at it and I do the subtraction in my head, what am I actually paying for the privilege. Then the monthly, because that's what hits the bank account.

**M:** And, um, do you trust the numbers?

**P2:** I trust the standard one because I know it. The rest, I already said. I don't know what they're assuming.

**M:** Right. Got it.

**P2:** And honestly, the other thing I don't trust is the payoff date. It says 2034 or whatever for me. But when I look at my last statement, and I've been making the regular payment, and the principal barely dropped, I think, how is it going to be 2034. I did my own math on my phone once and I got something later. But I might've done it wrong, I'm an electrician, not an accountant.

**M:** Did you check that against anything today?

**P2:** No. I'm just saying I wouldn't bet on it.

**M:** Okay. And, uh, is there anything you'd want to see differently?

**P2:** I already told you. Show what income it's using. Don't hide it in a little "i" thing. And, look, I'm not gonna sit here and tell you the page is bad. The page is okay. It's a table. It's the loan that's bad. If you could pass that along to whoever, that'd be great.

**M:** I'll, yeah. I will do that. Thanks so much, that's really helpful.

**P2:** Yeah. Sorry. I know I went off. It's a sore subject.

**M:** No, no, not at all. Take care.

[Session ends. Total time approximately 34 minutes.]
