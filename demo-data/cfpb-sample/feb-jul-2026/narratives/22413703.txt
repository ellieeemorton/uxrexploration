Regarding Repeated Discrepancies Between Borrower-Directed Payment Allocations and Actual Posted Allocations To Whom It May Concern : I am submitting this complaint regarding repeated discrepancies between borrower-directed payment allocations and the actual posted allocation of payments involving my institutional student loans serviced through Heartland XXXX On multiple occasions, I manually entered exact payment allocation amounts among my loan accounts using the servicers online payment interface. Prior to submission, the system accepted and displayed the allocations exactly as entered. However, after processing, the posted allocations materially differed from the borrower-directed instructions shown and accepted by the payment interface. Most recently, I submitted a {$9900.00} ACH payment with the following explicit allocation instructions : XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX XXXX After processing, the servicer instead applied the payment as follows : XXXX XXXX XXXX ( XXXX ) : {$4500.00} MCW Student Loan ( XXXX ) : {$5200.00} XXXX XXXX XXXX Loan ( XXXX ) : {$140.00} This redistribution materially altered the intended allocation strategy and diverted funds away from the higher-interest loans. Importantly, this is not an isolated occurrence. A substantially similar discrepancy has occurred previously. The repeated nature of these allocation mismatches raises concerns regarding the reliability and transparency of the payment allocation process and whether borrower-directed allocations entered through the online interface are actually being honored as represented. I preserved screenshots showing the allocation instructions as displayed and accepted by the payment interface prior to submission, along with account history reflecting the materially different posted allocation after processing. My primary concern is broader than the direct financial effect on my individual account. Many borrowers may reasonably rely on the online interface without independently auditing the final allocation after processing. If borrower-entered allocations are routinely overridden, ignored, or modified by backend servicing systems without clear disclosure, borrowers may unknowingly lose the ability to direct repayment strategy according to their own financial interests. This concern is particularly significant because : many borrowers may not understand amortization or interest optimization, many may not preserve documentation showing their submitted instructions, and even relatively small allocation discrepancies could compound materially over long repayment periods across large borrower populations.
