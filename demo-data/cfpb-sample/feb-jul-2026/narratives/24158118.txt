I am submitting this complaint based on new and continuing servicing conduct that occurred through Aidvantages XX/XX/XXXXXXXX final response to CFPB Complaint # XXXX. This complaint is not merely a repetition of my earlier complaint concerning the resumption of interest in XX/XX/XXXX. It concerns Aidvantages subsequent failure to answer a direct servicing question, failure to provide specifically requested account records, and continued collection and application of substantial payments without providing an adequate individualized accounting. My prior complaint expressly asked Aidvantage to answer whether Aidvantage itself sent me account-level notice that interest would begin accruing again on or around XX/XX/XXXXXXXX while my loans remained in SAVE-related forbearance. Aidvantages XX/XX/XXXX response did not answer yes or no. Instead, Aidvantage stated that Federal Student Aid allegedly sent a general email to affected borrowers and directed me to contact FSA. That response avoids the actual question. Aidvantage did not state that it sent me notice. It did not state that it did not send me notice. It did not identify any Aidvantage email, letter, account alert, portal message, telephone communication, or other borrower-facing notice concerning the interest-rate change. Aidvantage also failed to produce the documentation specifically requested in my complaint. It did not provide : 1. A copy of the alleged FSA email or any record demonstrating that it was sent to me. 2. The date, recipient address, transmission record, delivery status, or message identifier for the alleged notice. 3. A loan-by-loan accounting of interest accrued after XX/XX/XXXX. 4. A payment application history showing how my payments were divided between interest and principal. 5. A reconciliation of Aidvantages XX/XX/XXXXXXXX written representation that my interest rate would remain at 0.00 % and that interest would not accrue during the forbearance. 6. The contractual, regulatory, or servicing basis for Aidvantages position that it had no obligation to provide any account-level communication concerning this material change. Aidvantage therefore treated a general statement about an alleged third-party email as a substitute for answering an individualized servicing inquiry and producing records concerning my own account. Directing me to FSA does not explain what Aidvantage did, what records Aidvantage possesses, how Aidvantage implemented the interest change, or how Aidvantage applied my payments after the change. This failure is especially material because Aidvantage continues to service my loans, calculate and display my balances, assess interest, accept payments, and determine how those payments are applied. Over the past 12 months, I have paid more than {$20000.00} toward these loans in good faith. Aidvantage has continued accepting and applying those funds while failing to provide the requested accounting and while refusing to answer a basic question about its own borrower-facing communications. I am not refusing repayment and I am not seeking to avoid my financial obligations. My substantial payment history demonstrates the opposite. However, a borrower who has paid more than {$20000.00} in XXXX year should be able to obtain a clear explanation of how those payments were applied, how much interest accrued, what notice was provided, and how the servicer reconciles its current position with its previous written representations. The new issue is therefore Aidvantages ongoing failure to provide adequate account-level service and records after receiving a formal CFPB complaint specifically requesting that information. A nearly 60-day investigation followed by a generic referral to FSA is not a substantive response to an audit request. Aidvantages response left every material accounting question unanswered while the company continued collecting payments and assessing interest.
