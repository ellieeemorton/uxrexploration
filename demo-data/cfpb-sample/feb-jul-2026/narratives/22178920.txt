To the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau : I am filing a complaint against MOHELA regarding improper credit reporting and failure to provide adequate notice before reporting my student loan account as 90 days delinquent. MOHELA placed a 90-day late payment on my credit report, which severely damaged my credit score and financial standing. However, prior to reporting this delinquency, I did not receive any meaningful communication from MOHELA informing me that my account was past due or at risk of negative credit reporting. Specifically : * I did not receive email notifications warning me that my payment was overdue. * I did not receive phone calls regarding any missed payments. * I did not receive adequate written communication advising me that my account would be reported delinquent to the credit bureaus. * I was not given a fair opportunity to cure the alleged delinquency before the 90-day late mark was reported. MOHELAs own published credit reporting policies state that delinquency is reported once loans become 90 days past due, and that borrowers are contacted when accounts become delinquent. Federal law and regulations impose duties on student loan servicers to properly service loans, communicate with borrowers, and accurately report information to credit reporting agencies. Relevant laws and regulations include : 1. Fair Credit Reporting Act ( FCRA ), 15 U.S.C. 1681s-2 Furnishers of credit information, including loan servicers, are required to provide accurate information to consumer reporting agencies and investigate disputed information. ( [ Federal Reserve ] [ 2 ] ) 2. Regulation V, 12 CFR Part 1022 Implements the Fair Credit Reporting Act and governs responsibilities of entities furnishing information to credit bureaus. ( [ Federal Reserve ] [ 3 ] ) 3. 34 CFR 682.208 Due Diligence in Servicing a Loan Federal student loan servicers are required to exercise due diligence in servicing loans, including responding to borrower inquiries and properly managing repayment and delinquency communications. ( [ Legal Information Institute ] [ 4 ] ) 4. Consumer protection principles enforced by the CFPB prohibit unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices ( UDAAP ) under the Dodd-Frank Act when servicers fail to provide borrowers with adequate notice or engage in improper servicing conduct.
