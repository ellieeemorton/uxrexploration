Key Facts My loan is a federal Direct Unsubsidized loan The loan is not yet in repayment I made a voluntary payment intended to reduce accrued interest and/or principal MOHELA informed me that because the loan is not in repayment, the payment could be held for up to 90-120 days before being applied, during which time interest would continue to accrue -- - Federal Rules Clarification 1. Interest Accrual Is Legal Payment Delays Are Not While interest legally accrues on federal unsubsidized loans prior to repayment, servicers are required to promptly credit payments upon receipt. Holding a payment for months after receipt is not permitted and constitutes a servicing failure 1. 1. The 120 Day Rule Protects Borrowers It Does Not Permit Delays The 120 day rule applies only to payments made within 120 days of loan disbursement, requiring those payments to be treated as retroactive cancellations applied as of the original disbursement date. This rule does not authorize MOHELA to delay posting or processing payments and does not justify additional interest accrual after payment receipt 2. 1. Servicing Errors Must Be Corrected Federal guidance and CFPB oversight require servicers to : Properly apply voluntary payments Prevent interest accrual caused by servicer error or delay Correct accounts when improper interest is assessed due to delayed processing
