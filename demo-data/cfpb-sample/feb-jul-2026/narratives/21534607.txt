I am submitting this complaint regarding private student loans originated by XXXX and currently serviced by MOHELA. I requested a School Misconduct Discharge for my private loans and provided documentation and a detailed explanation of school misconduct and deception that caused me to take on these loans, including misrepresentations about program outcomes and costs. MOHELA responded that my loans are not eligible for its School Misconduct discharge program solely because the schools I attended ( XXXX XXXX XXXX and XXXX XXXX ) are nonprofit, and MOHELA provided no substantive analysis of my evidence. MOHELAs communications reflect what appears to be a blanket denial policy rather than a goodfaith, individualized review. In writing, MOHELA stated it confirmed my loans are not eligible due to the nonprofit status of the schools since the loans were originated, without addressing the misconduct facts presented, without identifying what evidence was reviewed, and without providing a reasoned explanation for denying relief on a misconduct claim. Unfair ( U ) : MOHELAs handling of my request is unfair because it appears to deny meaningful consideration of a misconductbased dispute and relies on a categorical eligibility rule ( nonprofit vs. forprofit ) that is not explained or disclosed in a transparent way to consumers. A process presented as a School Misconduct discharge program is effectively nonfunctional if misconduct evidence is not evaluated and denials provide no substantive basis. This causes substantial consumer injury ( continued collection on loans induced by misconduct ) that I can not reasonably avoid, and there is no countervailing benefit to consumers from denying individualized review. Deceptive ( D ) : MOHELAs written responses are deceptive or misleading because they imply a review ( Upon review, we confirmed ) while providing no details about what was reviewed, what standards were applied, or why the misconduct evidence is irrelevant. Representing that a School Misconduct discharge option exists while denying claims on a categorical basis without transparent criteria can mislead borrowers into believing there is a fair relief pathway when there may not be one in practice. Abusive ( A ) : This conduct may be abusive because MOHELA ( and XXXX as loan owner ) holds superior knowledge about eligibility criteria and internal decisioning, while borrowers can not access those standards. Denying relief without explaining the basis or the evidence considered prevents borrowers from understanding how to correct or supplement submissions and can materially interfere with the ability to exercise consumer rights ( including disputing debts and seeking misconductbased remedies ).
