Produced by a fresh-context subagent given only `themes.md` and `excerpts.jsonl` (no brief, no prior conversation), per `references/verification-subagent.md`. All findings below were independently re-checked against the raw data before being resolved. Every quote-fidelity and participant-count claim was also re-verified mechanically with `scripts/verify_quotes.py` and a standalone word-band recomputation after fixes were applied — both pass clean across all 51 citations and 17 Support/Counter-evidence lines in the current `themes.md`.

**Top-line from the subagent:** zero fabricated or altered quotes found anywhere (~35 quotes checked in the pre-fix draft). The real problems were one hard lexicon-word violation, one miscategorized counter-example, one overclaim that a single leading-induced response was the *only* density complaint in the dataset, and several citations in prose that weren't backed by the theme's own Support/Counter-evidence bracket list.

## Resolved findings

1. **Lexicon violation, estimator theme.** Counter-evidence was labeled "some (2 of 9)"; per the document's own lexicon, exactly 2 participants is "a couple." **Fixed:** relabeled "a couple (2 of 9)."

2. **Miscategorized counter-evidence, trust theme.** T4-L100 ("...which is how I know to trust the rest. Though, the rest I can't check, so, you see the problem.") was filed as counter-evidence on the reasoning that P4 names a flaw in his own logic. On replain reading, it affirms the exact pattern the theme describes (verify one number, extend trust to the rest) — a participant noticing his reasoning is shaky is not a participant who doesn't do it. **Fixed:** moved to Support (now co-cited with T4-L96 for P4). This drops the theme's real counter-evidence to one participant (P1, T1-L65), not two — Counter-evidence line changed from "a couple (2 of 9)" to "one (1 of 9)."

3. **Significant overclaim, H2 (density) theme.** The theme claimed P5's leading-induced "confusing" response was the *only* instance of anything resembling a density/overwhelm complaint in the dataset. T6-L40a ("Too many numbers.", unprompted, `leading_question_flag: false`) is a second one, already cited elsewhere in the document (the abandonment theme) but never checked against this theme's headline claim. **Fixed:** rewrote the theme to state precisely what's true — two complaints exist (P5's, about today's table, leading-induced and later reversed; P6's, about a past comparison, not repeated when he works through today's table), Support changed from "one (1 of 9)" to "a couple (2 of 9)," Counter-evidence recomputed from 8 to 7 participants (P8 moved out, see #6). Also softened the abandonment theme's closing claim that "none" of the four attribute abandonment to density — P6's "too many numbers" is hard to cleanly separate from a density complaint, and the theme now says so explicitly instead of explaining it away.

4. **Wrong ID, total-cost-engagement theme.** The exclusion note for P7 cited T7-L62 (about the income estimator) where it meant T7-L78 (his actual total-cost statement). Separately, prose claimed "P4 and P9 do detailed interest-cost arithmetic unprompted," but the P9 excerpt actually cited in that theme's Support bracket (T9-L48) is payoff-date arithmetic, not interest-cost arithmetic — the correct interest-cost excerpt for P9 is T9-L66b, which wasn't cited anywhere in the theme. **Fixed:** corrected the exclusion note to T7-L78; added T9-L66b to the Support bracket and quotes, and reworded the sentence to describe what each cited excerpt actually shows instead of a blanket characterization.

5. **Bracket/prose mismatches (citations discussed or quoted in prose but absent from the theme's own Support/Counter-evidence bracket list), several themes:**
   - Abandonment theme: P5's specific stated reason (overwhelm plus a new apartment lease) lives at T5-L28, not in the bracket (which only had T5-L20); P6's specific stated reason ("too many numbers" plus a work interruption) lives at T6-L40a/T6-L40b, not in the bracket (which only had T6-L36). **Fixed:** added all three IDs to the Support bracket (no change to the participant count — same four participants, now properly backing the specific claims made about them).
   - Stated-vs-observed theme: P7's stated intent ("I'd definitely use that...") is T7-L62, quoted in prose but absent from the bracket, which only counted the observed non-click (T7-L64). The theme is specifically about the *contradiction* between the two, so both halves belong in the count. **Fixed:** added T7-L62 to the Support bracket (no change to participant count — T7 already counted via T7-L64).
   - Sort-header theme: T3-L106 quoted in prose, absent from the bracket. **Fixed:** added (no count change).
   - Interest-rate-visibility theme: T6-L138 and T9-L84 quoted in prose, absent from the bracket. **Fixed:** added both (no count change).
   - Assumptions-disclosure theme (H3): the "complication" paragraph discusses T9-L74 (P9's capitalization-rooted distrust) in detail, but the Support bracket instead cited a thinner, more generic P9 excerpt (T9-L50, "projected is a joke... like a weatherman") that establishes relevance but not the specific point being made. **Fixed:** swapped T9-L50 for T9-L74 in the Support bracket and quote block, so the citation backs the exact claim the prose makes (no change to participant count — same participant).

6. **Miscategorization, estimator theme.** P5 was placed in the "did not discover the estimator" group on the strength of T5-L130, "I saw it. Didn't use it." — which says she *did* see it, directly contradicting that categorization. **Fixed:** reworded her inclusion to the pattern she actually fits — saw it, didn't use it until directly invited to, then complied rather than seeking it out (backed by the observed T5-L136, typing her income only after the moderator's explicit invitation). She stays in the theme's Support count (the theme covers both "never found it" and "wouldn't use it unprompted" as related sub-patterns), just correctly described.

7. **Overstated certainty, abandonment theme.** The theme's original title framed abandonment as simply "real." All four supporting excerpts are `self_reported_past_behavior` — a past session can't be directly observed, so "real" overstates the evidentiary footing available for a retrospective claim, even a plausible one. **Fixed:** retitled the theme to attribute the claim to what participants reported, and added an explicit note that this evidence type is inherently unobservable, not a shortcut the drafting missed.

## Second pass: researcher-directed self-audit (after the report draft, before sign-off)

The researcher asked, of the finished draft, "what might you see differently, and what was ambiguous that wasn't caught before." This is a different kind of check than the subagent's — it's not re-verifying that themes.md matches excerpts.jsonl (already confirmed clean), it's re-examining the original tagging and theme-construction judgment calls themselves, which nothing upstream of this point had independently challenged. One concrete error surfaced, plus several judgment calls worth documenting rather than hiding:

1. **Real error, fixed: a theme's title overclaimed its own content.** "Self-reported ease or clarity is repeatedly contradicted..." only accurately describes 2 of its 4 supporting participants (P3, P9). The other two (P4, P7) are real, verified say/do gaps, but about a stated *scanning order* and a stated *intent* respectively — not an ease or satisfaction rating. **Fixed:** retitled to "Self-reports — of ease, of intent, of scanning order — are repeatedly contradicted...", with an explicit note that this could instead be split into two more narrowly-scoped "a couple (2 of 9)" findings, left as the researcher's call.
2. **Checked and did not hold up, no fix made: suspected moderator contamination of P2's plan-naming confusion.** On rereading the raw transcript, P2's confusion ("Who names these") happens before and independently of the moderator's separate, later mockup mix-up — one doesn't cause the other. Worth checking, but the finding itself was already sound.
3. **Methodological transparency added, not a correctness fix:** cursor-dwell time (behind part of Finding 1) is a proxy for visual attention, not a direct record of it (no eye-tracking was used). The finding doesn't rest solely on this one inference, but the language describing it was softened from "directly contradicting" to "in tension with," and the inference is now named as an inference in both `themes.md` and `report.md`.
4. **Methodological transparency added:** the line between `observed_behavior` and `hypothetical_intent` for concurrent think-aloud (declarative narration vs. conditional "I'd do X" framing) is a consistently-applied rule, not a fact the data forces on its own — and it's a meaningful part of why P7 reads as unreliable throughout. Documented explicitly in `report.md`'s "what this data cannot tell us" rather than left implicit.
5. **Methodological transparency added:** the verification subagent (this file, above) checked that `themes.md` accurately represents `excerpts.jsonl` — it did not re-derive tags from the raw transcripts itself, so a debatable original tagging call (like #4) would pass that check regardless. Now stated explicitly rather than left to be assumed.
6. **Framing corrected for balance, not evidence:** P4's repeated restatements were framed mainly as a volume-caution/dismissal case, while P7's parallel say/do gap was framed more charitably ("his design critique may still be useful"). Both participants deserve the same treatment — weight the person's individual data lightly, but don't let that discount the substance of a critique that may independently be correct (P4's argument is a named anchoring effect, not just an opinion). Reworded both `themes.md` and `report.md` to apply the same standard to each.
7. **Recommendation separated from finding.** The Implications section's stance on recommender-vs-table investment was rewritten to state plainly what the data shows (a near-even split, with a shared transparency condition across both sides) separately from what is our judgment call on cost and risk (prioritize the cheaper, already-validated table fixes) — so a reader can't mistake the second for something the interviews decided on their own.
