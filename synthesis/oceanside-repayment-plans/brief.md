# Research Brief: Repayment Plan Comparison Tool — Oceanside Loan Servicing

## Background / what we know
Support tickets show a rise in borrowers calling in after using the online repayment plan comparison tool, asking questions the tool is supposed to answer. We don't yet know whether this is a usability problem with the tool itself, a trust problem with the numbers shown, or borrowers looking for reassurance regardless of how clear the tool is.

## Research questions
1. What do borrowers look at first, and in what order, when comparing repayment plans in the tool?
2. Where do borrowers get stuck, hesitate, or give up during comparison?
3. What do borrowers trust or distrust about the numbers the tool shows them?

## Hypotheses going in
- **H1:** Borrowers scan for the monthly payment amount first and pay little attention to total interest paid over the life of the loan.
- **H2:** The comparison table's density (many plans, many columns) causes borrowers to disengage before reaching the plan that best fits their situation.
- **H3:** Borrowers distrust the numbers shown because the tool doesn't explain how they're calculated, not because the numbers themselves are wrong.

## What we're looking for
Moderated sessions combining a think-aloud comparison task (observed behavior) with follow-up questions about trust and decision-making (self-report). Looking for both what borrowers actually do on screen and what they say about why, since those can diverge.

## How this changes our approach
- If **H2** holds → prioritize simplifying the comparison table (fewer default columns, progressive disclosure) over new features.
- If **H1** holds and total interest is genuinely being ignored → consider surfacing it more prominently, rather than assuming borrowers don't care.
- If **H3** holds → the fix is explanatory content next to the numbers, not a redesign of the numbers' presentation.
- Findings inform whether we build a **guided plan recommender** or invest in **improving the existing comparison table**.

## Decision this research informs
Whether to build a guided plan recommender or invest in improving the existing comparison table — and if the latter, whether the fix is simplification (H2), surfacing total interest (H1), or explanatory content about the numbers (H3).
